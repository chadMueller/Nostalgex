Quick answers.

Do I need a media server to use Nostalgex?

Yes. Nostalgex is a front end for a library you already have on Plex, Jellyfin, or Emby. If you run one of those, you're two minutes from channel surfing.

Do I pick what plays on each channel?

No, and that's on purpose. Nostalgex reads your library and builds the channels and schedules itself. The surprise of "oh, this is on" is the product.

Is the web tuner really free?

Yes. Open it in a browser, connect your server, and watch. It needs a server reachable over HTTPS, so a plain LAN setup is an Apple TV app job. That is where the full living-room experience lives anyway.

Does my data go through Nostalgex servers?

No. Playback and sign-in happen directly between your device and your media server. We never see your credentials or your library.

Is Nostalgex open source?

Yes. The Apple TV app, the web tuner, this site and the channel rules are all on GitHub under the MIT license. It's the same code that ships to the App Store. Found a bug or a file that won't play? Open an issue there. Want your own channel lineup? Fork it.

What's new

1.0.24OCT 2026

Emby libraries load, and the app tells you when something won't play

  • Emby libraries load again. The app was asking Emby for its libraries using Jellyfin's spelling of the address, and Emby answers that with a 404, so the scan failed on the very first request no matter what your libraries were called or how big they were. Every Emby user hit this. Thanks to Jay for the report.
  • When a programme won't play, the app now says why instead of quietly jumping to the next one. You get a card on the channel for a few seconds: whether your server refused it, couldn't keep up, couldn't find the file, or whether it's the Apple TV that can't decode it. It's amber when your server is the thing to go and look at, red when it's the file.
  • Finding out which of those it is meant asking your server directly, because Apple TV doesn't tell an app the status code behind a failed stream. So on a failure the app makes one small request to the stream that just died and reads the real answer.
  • Jellyfin and Emby step the quality down before giving up. If a stream starts falling behind, the app now asks your server for a smaller one and keeps the programme going. Plex has done this for a while; the other two were skipping straight to the next title on a single stall.
  • Error messages name the server you actually connected to. Jellyfin and Emby users were being told to go and check Plex settings they don't have.
  • The guide's vertical wrap is steadier. Rolling off the bottom of the channel list now comes back to the top reliably at any list length. Up out of channel one still goes to Settings, as before.
1.0.23OCT 2026

Halloween, Christmas, eleven new channels, and a guide that keeps up

  • Channel numbers have changed. Every package is now one unbroken run: NOSTALGEX 1 to 19, PRIME TIME 40 to 54, TIS THE SEASON 130 to 136, SCREAM 137 to 140. Music videos move to 200 and up. Packages used to have channels scattered across the lineup, and this leaves room for each one to grow without landing on another.
  • SCREAM arrives for October: FRIGHT NIGHT, SCREAM ADULTS, SCREAM KIDS and a new NOSTALGEX HORROR for 70s, 80s and 90s horror. The guide offers it rather than switching it on, nothing joins your lineup until you say yes, and saying no means it waits until next year. While it runs it sits at the top of the guide, with the kids channel first.
  • TIS THE SEASON is ready for December with seven channels. HOLIDAZE for everything, SANTA'S LIST for the kid-safe films, NAUGHTY LIST for the grown-up ones, NOSTALGEX CHRISTMAS for anything before 2000, HALLMARK for the TV movies, and two television channels: CHRISTMAS SPECIALS and KIDS CHRISTMAS TV, which find festive episodes of shows you already have by looking at the episode titles.
  • Both seasonal packages can be switched on any month of the year from Settings. The month only decides when the guide offers it and when it jumps to the top.
  • Four more channels elsewhere. HISTORY & BIO picks up the biopics and history films that only ever surfaced as new additions. DVD SHELF, Y2K COMEDY and Y2K SITCOMS cover 2000 to 2009, which the retro channels stopped short of. MILLENNIUM CARTOONS is now Y2K TOONS and sits with them on 19.
  • FRONT LINE is a war channel again. It was matching on words like battle and soldier without checking the genre, so Harry Potter, Pirates of the Caribbean and The Sound of Music were turning up between the war films.
  • HIGH ROTATION was asking for music videos from 1980 to 1999, and most libraries date them from whenever the file was made, so it was always empty. It now takes everything, and Y2K and CURRENT SPIN split the rest by era.
  • Music video channels fill in. Genres come from Deezer when the server has none, and the year is read from the filename instead of a server date that is usually just the day the file was added, so Y2K and CURRENT SPIN sort by the right decade.
  • Channels built into the app can no longer be replaced by collections found on your server. If you had collections turned on, a channel could be quietly taken over and show something unrelated.
  • Changing channels plays what the guide says. Tuning into a channel partway through a programme could start that programme from its first frame, or play the next one from the beginning. Jellyfin servers hit this on nearly every channel, because Jellyfin refuses a start time on a segment request. The app seeks on its own now, the way Jellyfin's own web player does.
  • Films where the server re-encodes both the video and the audio no longer lose the picture about 24 seconds in on tvOS 26. The app was asking Plex to adjust quality mid-stream, and that adaptive mode hands back segments slower than real time, which the Apple TV gives up on. Quality stepping is the app's own job now.
  • A stream the server cannot keep up with steps down to 1080p and carries on from where it was, instead of freezing on one frame or skipping to the next programme. A 4K file that transcodes slower than real time was doing both.
  • Dolby Vision films that Plex refuses to convert now play. When the server turns down the Dolby Vision colour space, the app asks once more for the same remux Plex's own Apple TV app gets.
  • The guide scrolls properly. Every channel's schedule was being rebuilt on the interface thread every thirty seconds, which froze scrolling for a moment each time, worst on Apple TV HD. That work now happens out of the way.
  • Jellyfin and Emby servers reached by hostname, VPN or Tailscale now connect over http. Only plain LAN addresses worked before, and everything else failed looking like the server was down. Typing just an IP finds port 8096, pasting the address from your browser cleans it up, and a failed connection says why.
  • Opening the app goes straight to your guide. If you were already signed in, the connect screen no longer appears first, and short waits show the logo instead of a progress bar with nothing to measure.
  • Settings now says how much of your library the app actually loaded, in titles, movies, episodes and shows. If that number looks far too small, that is worth telling us about, and it is a different problem from channels looking empty.
  • Settings also shows the last playback failure, under RESCAN LIBRARY. If something will not play, that line and the title count above it are what we need.
  • REWATCHABLES MOVIES and REWATCHABLES TV fill from Jellyfin and Emby play counts, the same way they already did for Plex. A title needs 3 or more plays.
  • Playback reporting works for Jellyfin and Emby too. It is still off until you turn it on in Settings, and a watch here only counts on your own server.
  • Audio follows the AirPlay speakers you already picked as the Apple TV's output.
  • From anywhere in the guide, Play goes full screen on what's already on. Menu jumps back to that channel, and again to leave the app. Settings is one press up in the package list, which is now wider and easier to read.

Channels play again

  • Fixed: many channels showed the tuning screen and never cleared, or played sound over a black picture. Anything your Plex server has to convert now starts in a couple of seconds on Apple TV running tvOS 26.
  • Fixed: flipping channels quickly, or the app retrying a stream, could get refused by the server. The app now asks the server first the way Plex's own players do.
  • Fixed: some HEVC MP4 files played audio with no picture. They now go through the server, which sends a version the Apple TV can show.
  • A file that opens but shows nothing now switches to the server stream instead of skipping the program.
  • Checked on both an Apple TV 4K and an older Apple TV HD against MKV, MP4, AVI, AV1, VC-1, 4K and HDR files: every file the server can serve played.

Stays signed in, opens instantly

  • Fixed: the app forgot your Plex sign-in after a force quit or restart. A fresh sign-in was never being saved (since May). Sign in once on this build and it sticks.
  • Fixed: every launch rebuilt the whole channel guide. The saved guide was never actually written on a real Apple TV, only in testing. Now the guide comes back instantly and refreshes quietly in the background.
  • Fixed: the Top Shelf on the Apple TV home screen never showed what's on now, for the same reason. It does now.
  • The 6-hour refresh asks your Plex server whether anything changed before rescanning. Nothing new, no rebuild. A full rescan still runs at least once a day.
  • Signing in again no longer discards the saved guide.
  • Tapping Connect retries a flaky plex.tv call and, if it still fails, says exactly why.
  • The connect screen shows a small diagnostic line. If you ever land there unexpectedly, a photo of it tells us what happened.
  • Settings text is bigger and brighter. Nothing under 20pt, subtitles at 80%, and the long explainers trimmed to one line.

  • A missing server list no longer counts as signed out. The app re-finds your servers from plex.tv on the next load.
  • A refused request (401) no longer signs you out after repeated tries. You see what Plex said and decide.
  • Tapping Connect retries a flaky plex.tv call a few times and, if it still fails, tells you exactly why instead of a generic connection error.
  • If a device ever does lose a sign-in, the connect screen now says so with a code to report.
